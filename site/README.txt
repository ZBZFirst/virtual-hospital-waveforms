Ventilator Waveform Observation Library

Original source: UC Davis Puritan Bennett Ventilator Waveform Data, https://zenodo.org/records/14884306, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14884306, GPL-3.0-or-later. See DATA-LICENSE.txt. The 145 byte-for-byte source CSV backups are in https://github.com/ZBZFirst/file/tree/master/WaveformBackups. This viewer fetches verified byte ranges from pinned GitHub commit 32063407e7fffb75d3866d080078d80295de476c.

The 10 Hz visual/audio track is derived read-only from the source CSVs by build_atlas_signals.py. It retains every fifth synchronized raw flow/pressure pair from the 50 Hz originals, without averaging. Flow is quantized on a fixed +/-60 L/min scale and pressure at 0.2 cmH2O per step; byte 255 means missing. build_signal_previews.py groups that unchanged track by active six-floor campus location into 30 gzip files under signal-previews/. The browser fetches only the selected floor group (or a selected recording's group) and uses original verified 50 Hz CSV samples for opened charts.

build_segment_index.py reads the original CSVs without modifying them. It writes a complete segment-index.json for verification and one small JSON shard per recording under feed-index/. The first core is 3,000 samples (60 seconds at 50 Hz); later cores contain up to 90,000 samples. Byte ranges include up to two minutes of neighboring context and nearby BS markers. All 145 opening ranges total 17,139,873 bytes (16.3 MiB), but the page requests only selected-floor opening ranges (at most six at a time, two concurrent) or the opened room. Each HTTP 206 response must match its indexed byte count and SHA-256 before parsing. The 2,048-bin overview comes from the per-feed index. Later ranges load ahead of playback or on seek. The original recordings remain at the pinned GitHub revision and are not rewritten. Run `node test-segment-store.js` and `node test-segmented-waveform.js` for range and sparse waveform checks.

The fixed outer ring uses signed flow: negative blue, positive red, near-zero gray, with a selectable reversal. The fixed inner circle uses pressure colors anchored at 1, 15, and 50 cmH2O. Database and unit nodes show arithmetic means of available room readings at the same session time. These colors are not clinical alarms.

Optional sound is off by default. Each enabled room has one Web Audio sawtooth voice with a low-pass filter, envelope gain, and shared master/limiter. Flow maps to a continuous pitch centered on MIDI note 60 with a +/-12 semitone span across +/-60 L/min; pressure maps independently to the numerical CC74 range 0-127 across 1-50 cmH2O and controls filter cutoff. No external MIDI hardware or Web MIDI connection is used. The voice uses the same 10 Hz atlas pair and playback position as the node. Multiple enabled feeds mix; Mute All releases every voice.

Calculated volume is derived from a room's loaded original 50 Hz samples. volume.js integrates signed L/min flow with trapezoids and converts to mL. BS begins a breath-relative trace at zero; BE certifies completion. Positive and negative areas are reported separately as VTi and VTe, and their difference is retained. A linearly interpolated zero crossing splits an interval between inspiration and expiration. The single CSV with per-sample timestamps uses those intervals; other feeds use 1/50 second. Missing, nonmonotonic, or >0.1-second timestamp intervals invalidate the affected breath. The original flow and pressure values and the 10 Hz atlas track are unchanged. Run `node test-volume.js` for synthetic integration checks.

breath-metrics.js adds completed-breath observational summaries from the loaded full-resolution samples. PIFR is the maximum positive flow in L/min. PEFR is the magnitude of the most negative flow after inspiratory flow has appeared. Peak pressure is the maximum pressure sample in the breath. PEEP is an estimate from the final five pressure samples after an expiratory phase; it is not a ventilator setting or intrinsic PEEP. MAP is mean airway pressure over the whole sampled breath, calculated as trapezoidal pressure-time area divided by elapsed sample time; it is not mean arterial pressure. It differs from ventMAP's implementation, which averages only inspiratory pressure. Run `node test-breath-metrics.js` for synthetic summary checks.

hospital-layout.js now generates the live atlas from active hospital-to-unit assignments. The database square is at the center, with one separate thick connection to each hospital card. Each hospital reserves all four floor positions even when a floor has no assigned unit; open floors show empty room and NS squares and can be selected to open their floor map. At most one unit occupies each floor. Each unit retains six patient-bed squares plus supply and break room squares around its NS position in the atlas overview. Hospital cards and corridors do not overlap, and empty beds remain visible. Hospital and floor names sit in separate canvas header bands above their room grids; at overview zoom, hospital names move to compact title boxes and floor names hide until they fit. Unassigned units are grouped into separate atlas cards until assigned. The older flat-unit geometry remains in the module for compatibility tests, but the viewer uses createHospitalAtlas. This is a display assignment, not a verified mapping to patients or physical rooms. Run `node test-hospital-atlas.js` for hospital grouping and edge geometry checks, and `node test-hospital-layout.js` for the older flat layout checks.

The page session starts at opening and runs at 1×. Every atlas node follows its recording at that elapsed position and stops at that recording's end. Unit and room views join the page session when opened; review controls can pause and seek independently. A completed feed stays selectable for Review Feed, and Replay feed or Replay real-time feed starts that feed again at zero. The room/unit assignments are virtual display labels, not confirmed patient or clinical location identities.

Hospital organization and floors: hospital-system.js keeps a source registry, derived feed metadata, stable unit definitions, hospital definitions, active bed placements, hospital-unit assignments, and four stable floor slots per hospital separate. The full CSV filename is the stable feed key because shorter opaque IDs repeat. The original manifest Room 1-6 beds are retained in their units; its Room 7-8 feeds fill six added units (20-25) in source order. This is the reset baseline. Each unit has six patient-bed positions; Rooms 7 and 8 are the supply and break rooms, and NS remains a non-patient nursing station. The 145 feeds now occupy 25 virtual units across seven virtual hospitals with at most four units each, initially one on each occupied floor. Hospital cards show unit previews, occupancy, and mean available 10 Hz atlas signal colors; these colors are not alarms. Selecting a hospital opens a four-floor selector and a floor plan; selecting a mapped room opens the existing waveform viewer. Hospital management allows creation, renaming, unit reassignment to a specific vacant floor, and leaving units unassigned. Bed sorting supports original order, recording duration, BS marker count, and manual placement, always previewed before application. Missing sorting metadata follows measured feeds by filename. An unassigned feed remains accessible in the room directory. Applying a layout changes only references and leaves raw CSVs, atlas signal offsets, and the currently open waveform data untouched.

The hospital detail screen shows the generated building, the selected floor plan, and that floor's six-bed flow/pressure waveform grid plus supply and break room tiles. Occupied tiles reuse the existing full-resolution 50 Hz segmented unit playback; unloaded tiles identify the first-10-second preview until their source segment is ready. Floor changes preserve the unit playback time, and open floors retain six empty beds and two shared rooms. The separate Bed Manager menu holds feed sorting, assignment previews, manual bed placement, reset, hospital creation/renaming, and unit-to-floor assignment. Layout controls are no longer embedded in the hospital overview or individual hospital screen.

The manifest records sample counts and BS marker counts from read-only CSV parsing. Duration is estimated as sample count divided by the verified 50 Hz source rate; it is not calculated from shifted filenames. Breath counts are BS start-marker counts, including a possible final incomplete breath, rather than completed-breath counts. Missing values remain unknown. Layout state is saved in browser localStorage under zebezo-hospital-layout-v3. Previous v1 and v2 layouts migrate on load; saved feeds in former Beds 7 and 8 move to the added units while preserving their feed identities. The original browser storage key is retained as a rollback copy. On first visit the original manifest layout is used. Saved layouts are validated before use; invalid layouts fall back to the original layout with an on-screen issue report and are not silently overwritten. The state model emits feed movement descriptions for later animation support, but this release has no movement animation. Run `node test-hospital-system.js` for assignment, capacity, persistence, and invalid-data checks.

Floor maps: floor-plan.js defines one deterministic schematic plan used on all occupied or open floor slots. Eight room rectangles, a nursing station, and a fixed elevator shaft have explicit doorway gaps in their wall segments. The nursing station has doors on all four sides, each joined to the hallway grid. An orthogonal hallway graph connects every room door and station to the elevator; cross-floor routes pass through adjacent elevator stops. Each room also has one explicit exterior facade and window position derived from its plan coordinates. The four-floor SVG building view stacks the floor slots, including vacant floors. South and east walls are shown in one view, north and west in the other, exposing all eight room windows on every floor. Selecting a window opens that room's floor map and elevator route. The floor map still opens the existing waveform view on request and switches floors through elevator controls or floor tabs. These are virtual navigational plans, not observed building geometry. Run `node test-floor-plan.js` for door, connectivity, window mapping, and elevator-route checks. Building and floor navigation do not rewrite source data or reset the page session clock.


Embedded PlayCanvas building: the library loads playcanvas-hospital/, a static PlayCanvas Engine build from the separate source project at ../playcanvas_hospital_prototype/. It renders one selected hospital with four physical floor slots and all eight room windows per floor. Occupied, empty, and unassigned-floor windows come from the same validated browser layout used by Bed Manager. Window picks select a room; the action opens the existing hospital floor plan and 50 Hz waveform grid in the library. Hospital selection, floor selection, orbit, pan, zoom, and browser storage refresh are active. This first prototype does not yet have a cloud PlayCanvas Editor project; source and Editor handoff details are in the prototype directory. Do not edit the generated playcanvas-hospital/assets/ files by hand. Run npm test and npm run build from the source directory to regenerate them.

On narrow screens, the atlas header keeps a Menu button visible and puts hospital controls in a side drawer. The drawer closes via its Close button, outside tap, Escape, or a selected action. The main page shows a centered Loading 3D hospitals state until the embedded PlayCanvas scene reports readiness. A load error or prolonged wait offers a scene reload; the recording-transfer progress remains separate at the bottom. The scene reports its status to the same-origin parent after its layout and interactions are ready.

In the embedded campus scene, the Building Inspector starts docked. Selecting a hospital updates the compact Inspector button without covering the building. Open that button to use floor, unit, and room controls; Dock panel, Escape, or touching the scene hides it again. This does not change feed assignments or waveform playback.

Room charts and unit waveform tiles start with a 10-second window. The room selector still allows 30, 60, or 120 seconds. On the PlayCanvas scene, one finger orbits, two fingers pan and pinch to zoom, and a tap selects a hospital, floor, Nursing station, or room. A gesture does not trigger a selection.

The unit and room detail overlay enters from the right with a short 3D rotation and slides back out when closed. Switching from a unit to a room replays the entry transition while keeping the existing playback handoff. Reduced-motion settings skip the animation.

Two-unit cube view: the Unit dashboard offers View on Cube. Cube A starts with the selected six-bed unit and Cube B with the next unit. Each face shows its assigned bed waveform using the manifest first-30-second 10 Hz preview; unit and face selectors can change what is shown without changing Bed Manager assignments. Drag to rotate and use Back to unit dashboard to return. The separate local ring_motion/cube_preview page starts with twelve different waveform faces, and its previous saved face configuration was backed up before updating it.

The standalone 3D full-screen page, its launcher, scripts, styles, and test have been removed. The PlayCanvas scene remains embedded behind the library dashboard.

GRID Monitor: double-click a cube face, or use its Open control, to open that recording's virtual room. At display resolution 1920x1080 or higher and browser viewport at least 1500x750, up to four independent draggable room windows pop out from the selected faces and tile in a 2x2 monitor grid. Each window has its own playhead, pause/start, scrub, flow, airway pressure, and calculated volume; verified 50 Hz source segments replace the 10 Hz preview when ready. Arrange grid restores the tile layout. Each Flow, Airway pressure, and Calculated volume Y axis keeps its observed bounds as the trace scrolls, expands for new extremes, and has Y−, Y+, and Y↺ controls to widen, narrow, or fit the current window. Full room opens the detailed individual room view. On smaller displays or viewports, double-click opens that ordinary room view directly. These are recorded virtual feeds, not live identified patients or clinical alarms.

Public cube view uses pointer drag for X/Y rotation and Shift-drag for Z rotation. Its two cube faces and GRID Monitor do not connect to local input hardware. Ring control remains in the separate local development source and Input Device Hub; no ring bridge code or controls are included in this public release.

The selected full-room Flow, Airway pressure, and optional Calculated volume charts use the same independent Y-axis controls and keep their observed ranges across Real-Time Feed and Review Feed for the selected room. A different room starts with new ranges.

This public release also retains the active selected-room Real-Time Feed and Review Feed views, source-breath navigation, browser research perspectives, and their supporting JavaScript and breath-sequence index from the 20261015 browser-room release.

Y-axis guide lines in the full-room live/review charts and GRID Monitor show adaptive numeric values for each chart. Labels follow that chart's locked range and its Y zoom/reset controls.

Room feed headers show a large Unit and Room title and a Patient ID parsed from the filename prefix before the first hyphen. Recording facts and dataset interpretation notes are in a collapsed details section below the charts; the full filename remains there for traceability.

The room feed selector and active playback controls share a single responsive toolbar. Live playback no longer displays a Browser Playback badge; the status remains available to assistive technology. Direct review links select the Review Feed tab.

The PlayCanvas floor-plan hallway uses nonoverlapping surface and border tiles at corridor junctions, preserving the existing path graph and room-door positions. Rebuilt from ../playcanvas_hospital_prototype/src/scene.js and hallway-geometry.js.

Patient-view browser sound now stops when the detail view closes, navigation leaves the room, or another patient is selected. An audio start still pending when the view is left cannot create a late voice; reopening a patient view permits sound to be enabled again.

Finite feed timing: a feed joins the browser page session at its current source time and stops at the recorded end. Closing and reopening its room advances from its last source position instead of restarting it. Faster Real-Time Feed playback advances that feed's shared position; after closing, it continues at 1× page-session time. Completed feeds remain assigned and selectable, with their unit tiles and cube faces marked complete. Review Feed remains available for seeking through the recording, and its Replay real-time feed button restarts that feed at zero. Replay feed in the live view does the same. These completion and replay states last for this browser page session; reloading the page starts a new session.

Controller layout: page/ and scene3d/parts/ retain the editable source scripts; build_runtime_bundles.py assembles page-runtime.js and scene3d/scene-runtime.js for delivery. index.html loads the page bundle, and scene3d/viewer.js imports vendored Three.js before loading the scene bundle. Campus bed placement: assign_campus_patients.py visits Hospitals A-E once per bed position and alternates independent UC Davis and MGH recordings until the 24 MGH recordings are assigned. The remaining occupied beds use UC Davis; 11 beds are empty. All 169 recording IDs remain unique and do not identify the illustrated patient. Rebuild signal-previews/ after changing assignments. In the scene, rooms with UC Davis ventilator data keep the ventilator and breathing circuit at the bedside while the unconnected vital-sign monitor is moved to a corner with its leads hidden. MGH rooms keep the vital-sign monitor connected while the ventilator is moved to a corner and its circuit and illustrated airway tube are hidden. Empty rooms show both devices inactive.

The walk-mode tablet uses an eight-second sweep. It draws each new trace column once and leaves completed columns in place until the sweep reaches them again. Source or room changes start a new trace; MGH cache refreshes do not clear completed lines. Run `node test-tablet-sweep.js` for this behavior.

Streamer mode has a full-page OBS layout switch. Horizontal uses a 16:9 capture stage for a 1920 × 1080 OBS Browser Source, and Portrait uses a 9:16 stage for a 1080 × 1920 Browser Source. The stage fits within smaller browser windows; the draggable webcam stays inside it and its orientation choice is saved locally. These dimensions must also be set in OBS to fill its source without letterboxing. Run `node test-streamer-layout.js` for orientation controls and camera shutdown.

The streamer webcam now uses the complete browser mask pipeline adapted from `webcam-mask-editor-v5.html`: five editable mask shapes, original/black/blurred/transparent/image backgrounds, image fitting, mirroring, feather/blur controls, microphone level, and recording of the clean composited canvas with available microphone audio. The draggable webcam window displays the cropped output; Edit mask opens the full-camera controls and outline preview.
